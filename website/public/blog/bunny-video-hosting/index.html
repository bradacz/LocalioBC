Bunny.net for website videos: prepare locally, deliver through a CDN

November 15, 2026 · The Localio Labs team

A short product clip or website header video needs two things: a sensibly sized file and a place to serve it. We use LocalioBC to prepare the file on macOS; Bunny Storage and CDN are a practical option for delivering it to a website.

Start with a smaller, purposeful video

Before choosing hosting, decide what the clip needs to show. Trim unnecessary seconds, crop to the space in your design and remove sound when it serves no purpose. In LocalioBC, you can adjust quality, limit resolution or choose a target file size, then export a finished MP4. Keep the original source for future changes.

A smaller file means less data for visitors to download. Check the result at the size it will appear on the page: fine detail that looks useful in a large preview may make little difference in a small product demonstration. The target-size estimate is a guide; the final result depends on the clip and export settings.

Why Bunny Storage fits this workflow

Bunny Storage stores the exported file. Connecting it to a Bunny CDN Pull Zone gives you a URL that you can use on your website. The CDN caches the file for delivery to visitors, while you keep control of the page and its video presentation. See the official Storage quickstart.

This is useful when you want to serve your own short MP4: a product loop, a website header asset or an interface demo. Upload the finished export and keep the URL with your website assets. Use a distinct filename when replacing a clip so your site can point explicitly to the new version.

Storage or Stream?

For a ready-made file and a direct CDN URL, start with Storage. Bunny Stream is a separate option when you want video encoding into several resolutions and Bunny Player. Choose the service around your playback needs. The Stream quickstart explains that workflow.

From LocalioBC to your website

  1. Prepare the clip on macOS. Trim, crop, remove audio if needed and export from LocalioBC.
  2. Set up Storage and CDN. In your own Bunny account, create a Storage Zone, select its primary region and connect a Pull Zone.
  3. Upload and use the link. Upload the exported file in the Bunny dashboard, then copy its CDN URL into your website.

We are preparing optional direct uploads to your own Bunny Storage account in LocalioBC. The currently published version 1.2.1 uses the manual upload workflow above. Editing and export remain available offline.

Keep an eye on hosting costs

The full direct-download edition of LocalioBC is free and open source, with no ads or watermarks. Bunny.net is an independent paid service: storage and CDN delivery have their own usage charges. Review the current Storage pricing and delivery costs before publishing a widely viewed video.
