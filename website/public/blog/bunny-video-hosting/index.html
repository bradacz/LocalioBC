Bunny.net for website header videos and CDN delivery

November 15, 2026 · The Localio Labs team

Bunny.net is a practical option for delivering videos in website headers and other page sections. Keep a short background clip, product loop or interface demo as your own file in Bunny Storage and serve it through a CDN. Prepare a smaller, cropped or silent version on macOS with LocalioBC first.

Deliver video for headers and other page sections

Bunny Storage stores your finished video file. A connected CDN Pull Zone gives you a URL for the header, product section or video component on your website. The CDN caches the file for delivery to visitors, while you keep control of the page design and playback presentation.

This suits a short header background, a product loop or an interface demonstration that you want to host as your own MP4. Keep the URL with your website assets, and use a distinct filename when replacing the clip so the page can point explicitly to its new version.

Storage or Stream?

For a ready-made file and a direct CDN URL, start with Storage. Bunny Stream is a separate option when you want video encoding into several resolutions and Bunny Player. Choose the service around your playback needs.

Prepare a smaller website video on macOS

Before choosing hosting, decide what the clip needs to show. Trim unnecessary seconds, crop to the space in your design and remove sound when it serves no purpose. In LocalioBC, you can adjust quality, limit resolution or choose a target file size, then export a finished MP4. Keep the original source for future changes.

A smaller file means less data for visitors to download. Check the result at the size it will appear on the page: fine detail that looks useful in a large preview may make little difference in a small product demonstration. The target-size estimate is a guide; the final result depends on the clip and export settings.

From LocalioBC to your website

  1. Prepare the clip on macOS. Trim, crop, remove audio if needed and export from LocalioBC.
  2. Set up Storage and CDN. In your own Bunny account, create a Storage Zone, select its primary region and connect a Pull Zone. Then enter the connection in LocalioBC Settings → Bunny Storage.
  3. Upload and use the link. Choose Upload to Bunny.net… for the finished export, review the files and destination, then start the upload. Copy its CDN URL into your website when complete.

LocalioBC 1.3.0 offers optional direct uploads to your own Bunny Storage account. Only the finished files you confirm are sent; editing and export remain available offline.

Keep an eye on hosting costs

The full direct-download edition of LocalioBC is free and open source, with no ads or watermarks. Bunny.net is an independent paid service: storage and CDN delivery have their own usage charges.
